# Erasmus+ Game — Story Concept

*Draft · 26 September 2026 · story stage, no code yet*

A cozy, Stardew Valley-style game about one week on an Erasmus+ school exchange.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Vinícius, 14, 9.º ano at Escola Secundária Almeida Garrett (ESAG), Vila Nova de Gaia |
| **Trip** | One-week Erasmus+ group exchange to Sarriguren (Navarre, Spain), hosted by IES Sarriguren |
| **Group** | 15 students (6 boys including Vinícius, 9 girls) + 2 teachers (1 man, 1 woman) |
| **Stay** | Host families (assumed) |
| **Working titles** | *Viagens na Terra dos Outros* · *Kaixo!* · *Onde o Vento Cruza as Estrelas* |

---

## 1. Premise

Vinícius is in 9.º ano at ESAG, in Vila Nova de Gaia. Next year he has to choose his secondary-school track, and he has no idea what he wants. Then ESAG announces an Erasmus+ exchange: one week in Sarriguren, a young eco-town 6 km from Pamplona.

Filling in the exchange's "Getting to know each other" form is how the player creates the character. Before the trip, Vinícius reads the same form from his exchange partner, Unai, and chats with him on eTwinning. Unai replies to everything with "jajaja".

The bus leaves Gaia at dawn. About ten hours later, plus one more because Spain's clock is an hour ahead, it pulls into IES Sarriguren. The host families are waiting with signs showing the students' names.

### The emotional core: saudade

The whole story is built on one Portuguese word, *saudade* (missing someone or somewhere). On Sunday Vinícius misses home. By Saturday he misses Sarriguren. On Wednesday Unai tells him Basque has its own word for the feeling: *herrimina*.

The week follows the usual culture-shock curve, squeezed into seven days: excitement, a low point on Wednesday, then feeling at home just before leaving.

## 2. The week

| When | Chapter | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Gaia | At ESAG he fills in his form (character creation) and reads Unai's. He packs a basketball and says goodbye to Mom and Dad at dawn. When the bus crosses into Spain, the clock jumps an hour |
| Sunday | *Kaixo!* | Evening arrival at IES Sarriguren. He meets the host family. Dinner is at 21:30 and he's been hungry since seven. The pea problem. His first mix-up with a Spanish word that looks Portuguese but means something else. A tarot card is waiting under his pillow |
| Monday | First day | Welcome at IES Sarriguren. He can't talk to anyone yet, but at break he can play basketball. He sits in a class taught in Basque and understands nothing. He plants seeds in the school's eco-garden and finds chickpeas in the canteen. The mayor welcomes the group at the town hall |
| Tuesday | Pamplona | The city walls and the star-shaped citadel, with viewpoints that reveal the map. The San Fermín bull-run route (no bulls). The yellow arrows of the Camino de Santiago (the pilgrim route). Pintxos and the gilda argument. The second riddle |
| Wednesday | Saudade | It rains. He and Unai misunderstand each other. There's drama in the Portuguese group chat, and his classmate Tiago wants to go home. The fix: cooking a francesinha for the host family. That night: "Wait, you read *Lord of Mysteries* too?" |
| Thursday | The wind | Trip to CENER or the Alto del Perdón wind farm. Tour of the eco-city with Unai's dad. Chess with Unai's grandfather. At night the cierzo, the region's strong wind, hits the eco-garden and the eco-house model. This is the *El Cierzo* boss fight, with Portuguese and Spanish kids working together |
| Friday | The final day | The eco-house model presentation, certificates, a mixed Portugal–Spain 3×3 basketball game, the mystery is solved, and a farewell party with all the families and Matilde's karaoke |
| Saturday | *Agur* | The bus leaves at dawn, and now it's Sarriguren he misses. He arrives in Gaia at night, with the Luís I bridge lit up and Mom and Dad waiting |
| Epilogue | Gaia again | He presents the trip to younger students at ESAG. Months later the Spanish group visits Gaia and he hosts Unai. If that's in June, it could be during the São João festival, with its famous plastic hammers. A final screen: "In four years you turn 18: DiscoverEU", the scheme that gives 18-year-olds a pass to travel Europe |

Each day ends with a diary page called *Viagens na Terra dos Outros* ("Travels in Other People's Land"). It's a play on *Viagens na Minha Terra*, a book by Almeida Garrett, the writer ESAG is named after.

## 3. Goals: the Erasmus+ passport

On his form, Vinícius wrote that he wants to meet people from other countries and get to know their culture, lifestyle and architecture. Those become the game's four goals.

Each goal has 3 stamps to collect in his Erasmus+ passport, 12 in total, like the 12 stars on the EU flag. The passport also echoes the stamp booklet pilgrims carry on the Camino.

| Goal | Stamps |
|---|---|
| People | Make a Spanish classmate laugh on purpose · Win the mixed 3×3 · Discover Unai's secret |
| Cultures | Learn 5 Basque words · Eat a gilda · Follow the Camino's yellow arrows through Pamplona. They're the same arrows pilgrims follow across the Luís I bridge in Gaia |
| Lifestyle | Survive a 21:30 dinner · Have *almuerzo* (the local mid-morning snack) · Go a whole day without the translator app |
| Architecture | Sketch Pamplona's star-shaped citadel · Tour the eco-city with Unai's dad, an architect who worked on it · Build the eco-house model for Friday |

The Friday project suits both schools. ESAG has held the Eco-Schools Green Flag every year since 2009–10, and Sarriguren is an eco-city, so this is an Eco-School visiting an eco-city.

## 6. The mystery: the Arcana Club

Inspired by *Lord of Mysteries*.

- **The first card.** On Sunday night Vinícius finds **The Fool** tarot card under his pillow, with a riddle in Spanish on the back. The classic Fool card shows a young traveller with a bag stepping into the unknown, with a small white dog at his heels. *Txuri* means "white" in Basque, and the host family's dog follows him everywhere.
- **The riddles.** Each riddle leads to a real place, such as the Camino arrows, the citadel, or the 13th-century Santa Engracia church. Each place unlocks a history entry, like Assassin's Creed's database. Examples: Pamplona was founded by the Roman general Pompey; Portugal and Spain joined the European Community on the same day, 1 January 1986; Sarriguren had only 10 inhabitants in 2000.
- **Hidden cards.** Every character has a secret card, revealed in their last friendship scene, like the Confidants in the Persona games. Each character starts out looking like a stereotype, and their card shows the real person.
- **The reveal.** On Thursday he works out who's behind it: Professora Helena and the Spanish teacher, Maite. They invented the club when *they* were 14, on an exchange of their own in the 1990s, and that's why this exchange exists. On Friday they give him the last card, **The World**, which means the journey is complete.
- **A school-safe version.** If tarot feels wrong for a school project, chess works the same way. Vinícius is a pawn crossing the board, and reaching the last row on Friday is his promotion.

## 8. Language

Spanish is easy for Portuguese speakers, so the challenge is **false friends**: words that look the same in both languages but mean different things. Each one is a funny scene to collect.

| Word | In Portuguese | In Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| esquisito / exquisito | weird | delicious (what the host mom hopes you'll say about her cooking) |
| presunto | ham | alleged |
| rato | mouse | a little while |
| acordar | to wake up | to agree |
| embaraçada / embarazada | embarrassed | pregnant |

**Basque** sounds like nothing he knows: *kaixo* (hello), *egun on* (good morning), *eskerrik asko* (thank you), *agur* (goodbye), *amona*/*aitona* (grandma/grandpa), *txuri* (white), *herrimina* (homesickness).

Using the translator app drains his phone battery, and every word he learns saves some.

## 9. Places

**Vila Nova de Gaia**
- ESAG, an Eco-School that has held the Green Flag every year since 2009–10.
- The Douro riverside and the Luís I bridge. Pilgrims on the Portuguese route of the Camino cross it into Porto.

**Sarriguren** (6 km east of Pamplona)
- The old village, with the 13th-century church of Santa Engracia. In 2000 Sarriguren had 10 inhabitants. Today it has about 16,000.
- The eco-city, planned by the Government of Navarre with bioclimatic design (buildings shaped around sun and climate). It won the European Urbanism Award in 2008 for sustainability.
- IES Sarriguren, which opened in 2019 and teaches in both Spanish and Basque.
- Lago de Sarriguren, the town's lake.
- The town hall. Sarriguren is the capital of the Valle de Egüés.
- The Parque de la Innovación, home to CENER, Spain's national renewable energy centre.

**Pamplona**: the city walls and the star-shaped citadel, the San Fermín bull-run route, the Camino arrows, pintxo bars.

**Excursion**: CENER, or the Alto del Perdón wind farm and its pilgrim monument.

## 10. How it fits the Erasmus+ 2021–2027 factsheet

- **Inclusion and diversity:** Rafael (rural background, worried about cost), Olena (migrant background) and Inês (dyslexia). What makes each of them different turns out to be a strength.
- **Green Erasmus+:** travelling by bus instead of flying (the factsheet's "lower-carbon transport"), an Eco-School visiting an eco-city, CENER and the wind farm, the eco-garden and the eco-house model.
- **Digital Erasmus+:** eTwinning before the trip, the translator-and-battery mechanic, the EU's Online Language Support (OLS) platform as the language app in the game, and the trip's Instagram.
- **Participation in democratic life:** the students present their ideas to the mayor.
- **The main character's growth** follows the factsheet's own wording: "confidence-building and independence", "understanding of other people", "a sense of European belonging".

## 11. Optional extras

- **The ghost of Erasmus of Rotterdam**, the Renaissance scholar the programme is named after, lives in the passport. He's grumpy: 500 years ago he turned down an invitation to Spain, writing *"Non placet Hispania"* ("Spain doesn't appeal to me"). Each day Vinícius shows him something new. By Friday he says *"Placet Hispania!"*
- **A motto.** The pilgrim monument at Alto del Perdón carries the words *"Donde se cruza el camino del viento con el de las estrellas"*: where the path of the wind crosses the path of the stars. The pilgrims show that people have always travelled, the wind stands for green energy, and the stars stand for Europe.

## 12. Notes

- Keep Vinícius's email and Instagram out of the game. If the game will be shared beyond family and school, use first names only.
- The classmates and teachers are made up. Only use real people's names with their permission.

## 13. Open questions

- When is the trip? ESAG's Erasmus+ page for 2026–27 group trips still says "em construção" (under construction).
- What's planned for each day of the real week? Each game day can then mirror it.
- Will the students stay with host families (as assumed here) or in a hostel?
- Will the Spanish group make a return visit to Gaia? That shapes the epilogue and a possible part 2.
